Matplotlib charts: line, bar, histogram, scatter, pie and box

Turn tables into pictures. Learn the 5-step recipe that every chart follows, then draw the six charts analysts use most (line, bar, histogram, scatter, pie and box plot) and learn which one answers which question.

In this chapter you will learn

  • Follow the 5-step recipe: figure, draw, label, tidy, show
  • Draw trends with plt.plot() and compare categories with plt.bar() / plt.barh(), also grouped and stacked
  • Show spread with plt.hist() and plt.boxplot(), and relationships with plt.scatter()
  • Show shares with plt.pie(), and pick the right chart for a question
  • Save a chart with plt.savefig()

The 5-step recipe for every chart

Matplotlib is Python's main charting library: almost every other chart tool, including Seaborn in Part 10, is built on it. You use its pyplot part, always imported as plt. Every chart follows the same five steps.

  1. Make the canvas: plt.figure(figsize=(width, height)), in inches.
  2. Draw: plt.plot(...), plt.bar(...) and so on.
  3. Label everything: plt.title(), plt.xlabel(), plt.ylabel(), and plt.legend() when there are several series.
  4. Tidy: plt.grid(alpha=0.3) for a light grid and plt.tight_layout() so nothing overlaps.
  5. Show: plt.show(), or save it with plt.savefig("chart.png").

Animated explainer: Building a chart step by step

  1. Step 1: plt.figure(figsize=(7, 3.5)) makes an empty canvas, 7 inches wide and 3.5 tall.
  2. Step 2: plt.plot(days, chai) draws a line: the days along the bottom (x), the cups up the side (y).
  3. Step 3: a title and two axis labels tell the reader what they are looking at. marker="o" adds a dot on every day.
  4. Steps 4 and 5: a light grid helps reading values, tight_layout() keeps labels inside, and plt.show() shows it. Done.

Bar charts: compare categories

A bar chart compares amounts across categories. plt.bar(names, values) draws upright bars; plt.barh(names, values) draws them across, which is easier to read when names are long or there are many of them. Sort the values first so the biggest stands out.

To compare two series per category, shift their x positions: a grouped bar chart. To show how two parts make a whole, put one on top of the other with bottom=: a stacked bar chart.

Animated explainer: Grouped bars: shifting the x positions

  1. One series, drawn at x = 0, 1, 2, 3 with width=0.4.
  2. Draw a second series at the same x and it hides the first one.
  3. Shift one left by half a bar (x - 0.2) and the other right (x + 0.2). Now they stand side by side, and plt.xticks(x, names) puts the names back in the middle.

  2. 3 4
  3. 1 9
  4. 9 3
Answer

3 9. plt.bar returns the bars. There are 3, and the second one (index 1) is 9 tall.

Histograms: how values are spread

A histogram cuts a number column into ranges (bins) and draws how many values fall in each. It shows the shape of the data: where most values are, how wide the spread is, and whether one side has a long tail (skew).

Most customers are 25 to 45, and there are more older customers than very young ones: the tail is a little longer on the right. Order values are much more skewed, because a few big Electronics orders stretch the right side.

OptionDoes
bins=15How many bars (or a list of edges)
density=TrueBar heights become shares, so the total area is 1
cumulative=TrueEach bar adds up everything before it
edgecolor="black"Lines between the bars, easier to read
Trap!

A histogram is not a bar chart. Bars touch, because the x-axis is a continuous number line. Use plt.bar for categories and plt.hist for a number column.

Scatter plots: are two numbers related?

A scatter plot draws one dot per row, with one number on each axis. A cloud going up to the right means the two rise together (a positive correlation, Chapter 26). s= sets the dot size, c= colours the dots by a third column, and cmap= picks the colour scale.

The dots rise to the right: bigger orders usually make more profit (correlation 0.78). The dots below 0 are the orders sold at a loss.

Predict the output

  1. 4
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 8
Answer

4. A scatter makes one collection of dots; get_offsets() holds one (x, y) pair per dot.

Pie charts: share of a whole

A pie chart shows each part's share of a total. autopct="%1.1f%%" writes the percent on each slice, startangle=90 starts at the top, and explode= pulls a slice out. Use it only for a few slices (about 5); for more, a bar chart is easier to read.

Easy tip

In "%1.1f%%", %1.1f means a number with 1 decimal and %% prints a real % sign. plt.axis("equal") keeps the pie perfectly round in older Matplotlib versions.

Box plots: spread and outliers

A box plot sums up a whole column in one shape, and is great for comparing groups. The box runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile (the IQR), the line inside is the median, the whiskers reach the furthest values within 1.5 × IQR, and dots beyond them are outliers.

Diagram: Reading a box plot. Half the values are inside the box; the dot on the right is an outlier.

Easy tip

patch_artist=True fills the boxes with colour, showmeans=True adds a marker for the mean, and vert=False lays the boxes flat. Name the boxes with plt.xticks([1, 2, 3, 4], names): the boxes stand at x = 1, 2, 3 ... (The course notes pass labels=names; Matplotlib 3.9 renamed it tick_labels.)

Slides: Chart types in 7 slides

  1. Pick the chart from the question.

    Time → line. Categories → bar. Spread → histogram or box. Two numbers → scatter. Shares → pie.

  2. Line: a trend

  3. Bar: compare

    • plt.bar(names, values)
    • plt.barh(names, values) for long names
    • Sort the values first

  4. Histogram: spread

    • plt.hist(column, bins=15)
    • Bars touch: it is a number line
    • Look for the peak and the tail

  5. Scatter: relationship

    • plt.scatter(x, y)
    • c=column, cmap="viridis" + plt.colorbar()
    • Up to the right = rise together

  6. Pie and box

    • plt.pie(values, labels=..., autopct="%1.1f%%"), few slices
    • plt.boxplot(groups): median, IQR, whiskers, outliers

  7. Always

    • A title that says what it shows
    • Axis labels with units: Sales (₹)
    • A legend when there are several series

Saving a chart: savefig

plt.savefig("name.png") saves the chart as a picture, for a report or an email. Call it before plt.show(), because showing the chart closes it. dpi= sets the sharpness and bbox_inches="tight" trims the empty border.

Trap!

plt.show() then plt.savefig() saves an empty picture: once a chart is shown, it is finished. Save first.

Key takeaways

  • Every chart: plt.figure(figsize=...), draw, title / xlabel / ylabel (and legend), grid / tight_layout, then show() or savefig().
  • Line for change over time; bar / barh for categories (grouped: shift x; stacked: bottom=).
  • Histogram (bins=) and box plot (median, IQR, whiskers, outliers) for spread; scatter (s=, c=, cmap=, colorbar) for two numbers.
  • Pie only for a few shares: autopct="%1.1f%%", startangle, explode.
  • Save before you show: plt.savefig("chart.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight").

Chapter quiz
  1. Which chart answers "how did sales change month by month"?
    1. Pie
    2. Line
    3. Histogram
    4. Box plot
    Answer
    Line. Change over time is a trend: a line chart.
  2. What does this print?
    1. 3
    2. 1
    3. 2
    4. 0
    Answer
    3. plt.bar draws one bar per value and returns them.
  3. You have 12 product names, some of them long. Which chart is easiest to read?
    1. plt.pie()
    2. plt.barh()
    3. plt.hist()
    4. plt.scatter()
    Answer
    plt.barh(). Horizontal bars leave room for long names; 12 slices would make a messy pie.
  4. What is the difference between plt.hist() and plt.bar()?
    1. None
    2. hist counts values in ranges of a number column; bar draws given heights for categories
    3. hist is sideways
    4. bar needs bins
    Answer
    hist counts values in ranges of a number column; bar draws given heights for categories. A histogram works out the bars from raw numbers; a bar chart draws the numbers you give it.
  5. What does this print?
    1. [2.0, 3.0, 1.0]
    2. [1.0, 2.0, 3.0]
    3. [3.0, 3.0]
    4. [2.0, 4.0]
    Answer
    [2.0, 3.0, 1.0]. 3 equal ranges of width 2: 1 to 3 holds 1 and 2, 3 to 5 holds 3, 4 and 4, and 5 to 7 holds 7.
  6. In a box plot, what does the line inside the box show?
    1. The mean
    2. The median
    3. The maximum
    4. An outlier
    Answer
    The median. The box is Q1 to Q3 and the line in it is the median (Q2).
  7. Your legend does not appear after plt.legend(). Why?
    1. The chart has no title
    2. No series has a label=
    3. Legends only work with bars
    4. You need plt.grid()
    Answer
    No series has a label=. The legend lists the labels you gave the series.
  8. Which line writes the percent with 1 decimal on every pie slice?
    1. plt.pie(v, percent=1)
    2. plt.pie(v, autopct="%1.1f%%")
    3. plt.pie(v, labels="%")
    4. plt.pie(v, fmt=".1f")
    Answer
    plt.pie(v, autopct="%1.1f%%"). autopct takes a format; %% prints the % sign.
  9. What does c=sales["Quantity"], cmap="viridis" do in plt.scatter()?
    1. Sets the dot size
    2. Colours each dot by its quantity, using the viridis colour scale
    3. Draws a line
    4. Removes outliers
    Answer
    Colours each dot by its quantity, using the viridis colour scale. plt.colorbar() then shows which colour means which quantity.
  10. Why can plt.savefig() after plt.show() give an empty picture?
    1. savefig is slow
    2. show() finishes and closes the chart, so there is nothing left to save
    3. PNG is not supported
    4. It needs dpi
    Answer
    show() finishes and closes the chart, so there is nothing left to save. Save first, then show.
