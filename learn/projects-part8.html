Part 8 projects: data analysis with Pandas

Three everyday analyst jobs on CSV files: clean a messy HR staff export, build a monthly sales report, and analyse student results across two files. Read each file with Pandas, clean it, then let groupby, filters and merge do the work.

In the interactive version you write each program in the browser, run it with your own numbers, and press Submit to get an automatic score out of 100 with feedback. A score of 70 or more means you are ready for the next project.

Project 8.1 (Beginner): Clean an HR staff file

Read a messy staff export, clean it with Pandas and print a short HR report.

Lakshmi works in HR at an IT company in Hyderabad. Every month the payroll system exports staff.csv, and every month it is messy: names in random capitals with extra spaces, departments typed three different ways, a few missing salaries and cities, and sometimes the same row twice.

Write a program that reads the file with Pandas, cleans it, and prints a short report she can paste into her email.

What you will practise

  • Read a CSV file with pd.read_csv()
  • Remove repeated rows with drop_duplicates()
  • Tidy text with .str.strip(), .str.title() and .str.upper()
  • Fill gaps with fillna() (the median salary, and "Unknown" for cities)
  • Summarise with mean(), idxmax() and groupby()

You may use: everything from Parts 1-7, Pandas: read_csv, drop_duplicates, .str tools, isna, fillna, median, mean, unique, idxmax, loc, groupby.
Do not use yet: Matplotlib or Seaborn charts (Parts 9 and 10).

Your tasks

  1. The file is called staff.csv and has the columns emp_id, name, department, salary and city. Read it with pd.read_csv.
  2. If the file has no rows, print No staff in the file and stop. Otherwise print Rows read: <n>.
  3. Remove exact duplicate rows and print Duplicates removed: <n>.
  4. Tidy the text: names with strip() then title(); departments with strip() then upper().
  5. Count the missing salaries, fill them with the median salary (of the rows left after removing duplicates) and print Missing salaries filled: <n>. Then fill missing cities with Unknown and print Missing cities filled: <n>.
  6. Print Departments: and the department names sorted A to Z, separated by , .
  7. Print the Average salary, and the Highest paid person with their salary (on a tie, the one higher up in the file).
  8. For each department, A to Z, print <DEPT>: <n> staff, average ₹<amount>.

Lines your output must contain

Rows read: <n>
Duplicates removed: <n>
Missing salaries filled: <n>
Missing cities filled: <n>
Departments: FINANCE, HR, IT
Average salary: ₹<amount>
Highest paid: <Name> (₹<amount>)
<DEPT>: <n> staff, average ₹<amount>
No staff in the file

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals, like ₹56,250.00. Names are in title case (Asha Rao) and departments in capitals (IT).

Edge cases to think about

  • A file with only the header row: no staff at all.
  • A file where every row is there twice.
  • No gaps at all: 0 salaries and 0 cities filled.
  • Two people with the same top salary: the one higher up in the file.
  • An even number of salaries: the median is the average of the two middle ones.

Project 8.2 (Intermediate): Monthly sales report

Read a year of orders, ask for a month and print that month's sales report.

Rahul manages a chain of stores across India. The till system exports orders.csv: one row per order with the date, the category, the region, the sales and the profit in rupees. The dates come in three styles, and a few are not dates at all.

At the start of every month Rahul wants a report for the month he types in: totals, margin, the categories with their share of sales, the best region and the best day.

What you will practise

  • Turn mixed text dates into real dates with pd.to_datetime() and drop the bad ones
  • Check a typed month and filter the rows with a mask
  • Total, group and sort with sum(), groupby() and sort_values()
  • Find the best region and the best day with idxmax()

You may use: everything from Parts 1-7, Pandas: read_csv, to_datetime, dropna, .dt, masks, groupby, sum, sort_values, idxmax, reset_index.
Do not use yet: Matplotlib or Seaborn charts (Parts 9 and 10).

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv (columns Date, Category, Region, Sales, Profit). Convert Date with format="mixed", dayfirst=True and errors="coerce".
  2. Count the dates that could not be read, drop those rows and print Bad dates skipped: <n>.
  3. Ask Month (1-12): . If it is not a whole number from 1 to 12, print Please enter a month from 1 to 12 and stop.
  4. Keep only that month's orders. If there are none, print No orders in <Month> (the full month name) and stop.
  5. Print Report for <Month>, Orders: <n>, Total sales, Total profit and Margin: <x.x>% (profit ÷ sales × 100).
  6. For each category, largest sales first (a tie: A to Z), print <Category>: ₹<sales> (<x.x>% of sales).
  7. Print the Best region with its sales, and the Best day (the date with the most sales, written like 02 Nov 2024) with its sales. On a tie, take the region A to Z first and the earlier day.
  8. Print Orders at a loss: <n>: the orders of the month with a profit below 0.

Lines your output must contain

Bad dates skipped: <n>

Orders: <n>
Total sales: ₹<amount>

Best day: <dd Mon yyyy> (₹<amount>)

Please enter a month from 1 to 12

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. Percents have 1 decimal. Month names are in full (November). Dates in 05/11/2024 style are day first.

Project 8.3 (Challenging): Student results analysis

Combine marks and house lists, grade every student and compare classes and houses.

Mrs Iyer is the exams coordinator at a school in Vijayawada. After the term exams she has two files. marks.csv has every student's marks in maths, science and English (a blank means the student was absent). students.csv says which house each student belongs to, but it is kept by another teacher, so a few students are missing and one may be listed twice.

She wants one report: pass counts, grades, a line for every class with its topper, the students with no house, and the houses ranked by their average.

What you will practise

  • Read two CSV files and fill blank marks with fillna(0)
  • Add columns with maths and with your own function through apply()
  • Summarise each class with groupby() and find each topper with idxmax()
  • Join the two files with merge(), after removing repeated ids, and find unmatched rows with isna()

You may use: everything from Parts 1-7, all of Part 8: read_csv, fillna, apply, np.where, masks, groupby, agg, idxmax, drop_duplicates, merge, isna, sort_values.
Do not use yet: Matplotlib or Seaborn charts (Parts 9 and 10).

Your tasks

  1. Read marks.csv (student_id, name, class, maths, science, english) and students.csv (student_id, house). If marks.csv has no rows, print No students in marks.csv and stop.
  2. Print Students: <n> and Absent marks: <n> (the number of blank mark cells). Then treat a blank mark as 0.
  3. Add percent = (maths + science + english) ÷ 3, and passed = True when every subject is 35 or more. Print Passed: <p> of <n> (<x.x>%).
  4. Write def grade(percent): A+ for 90 or more, A for 75 or more, B for 60 or more, C for 40 or more, otherwise F. Add it as a column with apply and print Grades: A+ <n>, A <n>, B <n>, C <n>, F <n> (always all five, in this order).
  5. For each class, A to Z, print Class <c>: <n> students, average <x.x>%, pass rate <x.x>%, topper <name> (<x.x>%). On a tie, the topper is the one higher up in the file.
  6. Remove repeated student_ids from students.csv, then merge it onto the marks with how="left". Print Missing house: and the ids with no house, in file order, separated by , (or none).
  7. Print House <name>: <x.x>% (the average percent) for every house, highest first; on a tie, A to Z.

Lines your output must contain

Students: <n>
Absent marks: <n>
Passed: <p> of <n> (<x.x>%)
Grades: A+ <n>, A <n>, B <n>, C <n>, F <n>
Class <c>: <n> students, average <x.x>%, pass rate <x.x>%, topper <name> (<x.x>%)
Missing house: <ids> or none
House <name>: <x.x>%
No students in marks.csv

Percents have 1 decimal and a % sign. A blank mark counts as 0 and as absent. Classes are A to Z; houses are highest average first.

Edge cases to think about

  • A student who was absent for every subject (0%).
  • A student who is in marks.csv but not in students.csv.
  • A student id listed twice in students.csv: after a merge they would count twice.
  • A mark of exactly 35 passes; 34 fails.
  • A grade with nobody in it still shows 0.
  • Two students with the same top percent in a class.
