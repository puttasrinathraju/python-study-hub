Part 10 projects: statistical charts with Seaborn

Three reports built with Seaborn: the shape of the order values, a category comparison for any region, and a full insights report with a correlation heatmap and a panel per region. Pandas does the numbers, Seaborn draws them, and the checker reads your charts.

Project 10.1 (Beginner): The shape of order values

Median, average and big orders, plus a Seaborn histogram with a smooth curve.

Priya runs an online home store. Before she plans next year's offers she wants to know what a typical order looks like: is it the average, or are a few big orders pulling the average up? Give her the numbers and one clean Seaborn histogram she can show the team.

What you will practise

  • Read a CSV file and handle an empty one
  • Compare the median and the mean to spot a long tail
  • Count and share the orders above a limit
  • Draw a Seaborn histogram with kde=True, a title and axis labels, and save it

You may use: everything from Parts 1-9, Seaborn: sns.set_theme, sns.histplot (bins=, kde=True), plt.title, plt.xlabel, plt.ylabel, plt.savefig, plt.show.
Do not use yet: Other chart libraries (Plotly, Bokeh): not part of this course.

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv (Date, Category, Region, Sales, Profit, Quantity). If it has no rows, print No orders in the file and stop (draw nothing).
  2. Print Orders: <n>, the Median order and the Average order of Sales.
  3. Count the orders over ₹5,000 (more than 5000) and print them with their share of all orders: Orders over ₹5,000: 7 (17.5%).
  4. Print Long tail of big orders: Yes when the average is above the median, otherwise Long tail of big orders: No.
  5. Switch on a theme with sns.set_theme(style="whitegrid"), then draw one Seaborn histogram of Sales with bins=15 and kde=True. Title Order values, x label Sales (₹), y label Orders.
  6. Save it as order_values.png, print Saved order_values.png, then plt.show().

Lines your output must contain

Orders: <n>
Median order: ₹<amount>
Average order: ₹<amount>
Orders over ₹5,000: <n> (<x.x>%)
Long tail of big orders: Yes
Long tail of big orders: No
Saved order_values.png
No orders in the file
Chart report (checked after your program): Figure 1: 1 chart
Chart 1.1: bar+line, 15 bars, 1 line, title: Order values, x: Sales (₹), y: Orders

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals; the share has 1 decimal. Seaborn draws the bins as bars and the KDE as one line, which is what the chart report shows.

Project 10.2 (Intermediate): Category comparison for any region

Pick a region, rank the categories by average order, and draw bars and box plots side by side.

Each regional manager wants the same picture for their own region: which categories bring the biggest orders, and how much the profit of single orders swings. The program asks for a region (or All), checks it, and builds the two charts in one figure.

What you will practise

  • Clean and check a typed name against the data
  • Rank categories by their average with groupby and sort_values
  • Put Seaborn charts into a Matplotlib grid with ax=
  • Keep both charts in the same category order

You may use: everything from Parts 1-9, Seaborn: sns.barplot (order=, errorbar=None, ax=), sns.boxplot (order=, ax=), plt.subplots, ax.set(...), fig.suptitle, plt.tight_layout.
Do not use yet: Other chart libraries (Plotly, Bokeh): not part of this course.

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv. Ask Region (or All): and clean the answer with .strip().title() (so north becomes North).
  2. If it is not All and not a region in the file, print Unknown region: <name> and Regions: <the regions in the file, A to Z, joined by ", ">, and stop.
  3. Keep the orders of that region (All keeps every order). Print Region: <name> and Orders: <n>.
  4. Work out the average Sales per Category and sort it from high to low (a tie: A to Z). Print the Highest average order and the Lowest average order with their categories and amounts.
  5. Print Loss-making orders: <n> (profit below 0).
  6. Make fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4)). Left: sns.barplot of the average Sales per Category in the sorted order, no error bars, title Average order value, y label Sales (₹).
  7. Right: sns.boxplot of Profit per Category in the same order, title Profit per order, y label Profit (₹). Keep the x labels Seaborn sets (Category).
  8. Give the figure the title Category comparison: <name>, call plt.tight_layout() and show it.

Lines your output must contain

Region: <name>
Orders: <n>
Highest average order: <Category> (₹<amount>)
Lowest average order: <Category> (₹<amount>)
Loss-making orders: <n>
Unknown region: <name>
Regions: East, North, South, West
Chart report: Figure 1: 2 charts, title: Category comparison: <name>
Chart 1.1: bar, <n> bars, title: Average order value, x: Category, y: Sales (₹)
Chart 1.2: area+box, <n> boxes, title: Profit per order, x: Category, y: Profit (₹)

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. The chart report shows what Seaborn draws: a box plot also adds a small area part, so area+box is expected.

Project 10.3 (Challenging): Sales insights report

Find the strongest link, the profit per rupee and the best margins, with a heatmap and a panel per region.

The finance head asks three questions every quarter: which numbers move together, how much profit each extra rupee of sales brings, and which region keeps the most of what it sells. Answer them in print, then back them up with a correlation heatmap and one scatter panel per region.

What you will practise

  • Build a correlation table and find the strongest pair by absolute value
  • Fit a straight line with np.polyfit and explain the slope
  • Work out margins per region from totals
  • Draw a heatmap and a figure-level relplot with panels, titles and labels, and save it

You may use: everything from Parts 1-9 (NumPy and Pandas included), all of Part 10: sns.heatmap, sns.relplot (col=, col_wrap=, col_order=, hue=), g.set_titles, g.set_axis_labels, g.figure.suptitle, g.savefig.
Do not use yet: Other chart libraries (Plotly, Bokeh): not part of this course.

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv. If it has fewer than 3 orders, print Need at least 3 orders and stop (no chart). Otherwise print Orders: <n>.
  2. Make the correlation table of Sales, Profit and Quantity. Of the three pairs (Sales and Profit, Sales and Quantity, Profit and Quantity, in this order), find the one with the biggest correlation ignoring the sign (a tie: the first). Print it with its real value, 2 decimals: Strongest link: Sales and Profit (0.78).
  3. Fit Profit against Sales with np.polyfit(x, y, 1) and print the slope: Profit per ₹1 of sales: ₹0.15.
  4. For each region, the margin is its total profit ÷ its total sales × 100. Print Best margin: <Region> (<x.x>%) and Worst margin: <Region> (<x.x>%) (a tie: A to Z).
  5. Figure 1: sns.heatmap of the correlation table with the values (annot=True, fmt=".2f"), cmap="coolwarm", center=0, vmin=-1, vmax=1, and the title Correlations.
  6. Figure 2: sns.relplot of Sales (x) against Profit (y), coloured by Category, one panel per Region with col_wrap=2, in the order North, South, East, West (only the regions in the file).
  7. On the grid: g.set_titles("{col_name}"), g.set_axis_labels("Sales (₹)", "Profit (₹)") and g.figure.suptitle("Sales vs profit by region", y=1.03). Save it with g.savefig("insights.png"), print Saved insights.png, then plt.show().

Lines your output must contain

Orders: <n>
Strongest link: <col> and <col> (<x.xx>)
Profit per ₹1 of sales: ₹<x.xx>
Best margin: <Region> (<x.x>%)
Worst margin: <Region> (<x.x>%)
Saved insights.png
Need at least 3 orders
Chart report: Figure 1: 1 chart
Chart 1.1: mesh, 1 heatmap, title: Correlations
Figure 2: <n> charts, title: Sales vs profit by region
Chart 2.1: scatter, <n> points, title: North, y: Profit (₹)
... one line per panel

The correlation keeps its sign (for example -0.41). In a FacetGrid only the bottom panels show the x label and only the left panels the y label, so the report lists the labels like that. Margins have 1 decimal; the slope 2 decimals.
