Part 3 projects: text and collections

Three data-cleaning and summary jobs that analysts do every week: tidy a messy list, group sales by region, and find the main words in customer feedback. They use strings, lists, sets, dictionaries and comprehensions, but no functions yet.

In the interactive version you write each program in the browser, run it with your own numbers, and press Submit to get an automatic score out of 100 with feedback. A score of 70 or more means you are ready for the next project.

Project 3.1 (Beginner): Clean a customer list

Turn a messy, pasted list of names into a clean customer list.

Priya works in the marketing team of a jewellery store in Chennai. Sales staff send her customer names copied from WhatsApp, paper slips and old Excel files. The list is a mess: random capitals, extra spaces, empty entries between two commas, and the same customer typed twice in different ways.

Write a program that reads the whole list in one line, cleans every name, removes the duplicates, and prints a short cleaning report with the final list.

What you will practise

  • Split one line of text into a list
  • Clean text with strip(), split(), join() and title()
  • Remove duplicates but keep the first-seen order
  • Report how much dirty data you removed

You may use: everything from Parts 1 and 2, string methods, lists and list comprehensions, sets, len(), sorted().
Do not use yet: your own functions (def, lambda), try / except, import, files.

Your tasks

  1. Ask once with input(): Paste the names, separated by commas: and split the text at every comma.
  2. Raw entries is the number of pieces after splitting.
  3. An entry that is empty or only spaces is an empty entry: count it and drop it.
  4. Clean every other entry: remove spaces at both ends, turn several spaces inside a name into one, and use title case (Kiran Kumar).
  5. Remove duplicates: after cleaning, asha and ASHA are the same customer. Keep the first one you see, in the original order. Count how many you removed.
  6. If no customers are left, print No customers after the counts.
  7. Otherwise print the customers in first-seen order, then sorted A to Z, both joined with , .
  8. Print the longest name (counting every character, spaces too). If two names are equally long, keep the first one.

Lines your output must contain

Raw entries: <count>
Empty entries: <count>
Duplicates removed: <count>
Unique customers: <count>
Customers: <name>, <name>, ...
Sorted: <name>, <name>, ...
Longest name: <name>
No customers

Counts are plain whole numbers. Names are joined with a comma and one space. Print No customers only when nothing is left, and in that case skip the last three lines.

Example run

Typed values: asha ,RAVI, neha,, Asha , kiran kumar.

Paste the names, separated by commas:  asha ,RAVI, neha,, Asha , kiran kumar
Raw entries: 6
Empty entries: 1
Duplicates removed: 1
Unique customers: 4
Customers: Asha, Ravi, Neha, Kiran Kumar
Sorted: Asha, Kiran Kumar, Neha, Ravi
Longest name: Kiran Kumar

Edge cases to think about

  • Two commas in a row (asha,,ravi) or a piece with only spaces: that is an empty entry.
  • The same name in different capitals (ASHA, asha, Asha): one customer.
  • Several spaces inside a name (ravi kumar): clean it to Ravi Kumar, so it matches Ravi Kumar.
  • Only commas and spaces: no customers at all.
  • Names of the same length: the first one stays the longest.

Hint 1

pieces = text.split(",") gives the raw entries. Then loop over them: if piece.strip() == "": counts an empty entry; otherwise clean it.

Hint 2

To squeeze spaces inside a name, split it into words and join them again: " ".join(piece.split()). Then add .title().

Project 3.2 (Intermediate): Sales by region

Group raw sales rows by region, like a pivot table, and rank the regions.

Arjun is a sales analyst for a chain of mobile phone stores across Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. Every evening the store managers message him their sales as rows like North, 1200. Some rows are broken: a missing comma, a word instead of a number, or extra commas.

Write a program that reads the rows until he types done, skips the broken ones, adds up the sales per region (a GROUP BY, like a pivot table in Excel), and prints a ranked summary with each region's share of the total.

What you will practise

  • Parse and check rows of text before using them
  • Group and count with a dictionary
  • Rank dictionary keys by their values, with a fair tie rule
  • Work out shares (percentages) safely

You may use: everything from Parts 1 and 2, string methods, lists, tuples, sets, dictionaries and get(), sorted() with key=, comprehensions.
Do not use yet: your own functions (def, lambda), try / except, import, files.

Your tasks

  1. In a loop, ask Sale (region,amount) or done: . Stop when the user types done (any capitals, spaces around it are fine).
  2. A row is valid only if it splits into exactly 2 parts at the comma, the region is not empty, and the amount (after strip()) is made only of digits. Amounts are whole rupees.
  3. Skip broken rows and count them. Clean the region name: no extra spaces and title case, so north and NORTH are both North.
  4. For valid rows, add the amount to that region's total and count the sale, using dictionaries.
  5. After the loop, print Valid sales and Skipped rows. If there were no valid sales, print No valid sales and stop.
  6. Rank the regions by total, highest first. If two regions have the same total, put them in A to Z order.
  7. Print one line per region with its rank: 1. North: ₹3,200 (3 sales, 45.7%). Use 1 sale for a single sale. The share is the region total ÷ the grand total × 100, with 1 decimal. If the grand total is 0, every share is 0.0%.
  8. Print Total: ₹<amount> and Top region: <name>.

Lines your output must contain

Valid sales: <count>
Skipped rows: <count>
<rank>. <Region>: ₹<amount> (<n> sales, <share>%)
Total: ₹<amount>
Top region: <Region>
No valid sales

Amounts use commas (:,), like ₹3,200. The share has 1 decimal (:.1f) and a % sign. Write 1 sale but 2 sales. Print No valid sales instead of the region lines when nothing was valid.

Example run

Typed values: North, 1200, South,800, north,1500, East, 1700, South, 1300, North,500, done.

Sale (region,amount) or done: South,800
Sale (region,amount) or done: north,1500
Sale (region,amount) or done: East, 1700
Sale (region,amount) or done: South, 1300
Sale (region,amount) or done: North,500
Sale (region,amount) or done: done
Valid sales: 6
Skipped rows: 0
1. North: ₹3,200 (3 sales, 45.7%)
2. South: ₹2,100 (2 sales, 30.0%)
3. East: ₹1,700 (1 sale, 24.3%)
Total: ₹7,000
Top region: North

Edge cases to think about

  • Broken rows: bad line (no comma), North,abc (not a number), North,1,200 (3 parts) and ,500 (no region) are all skipped.
  • The same region in different capitals or spaces is one region.
  • Ties: two regions with the same total are ranked A to Z.
  • No valid rows at all: no ranking, no division.
  • Every amount is ₹0: the total is 0, so the shares must not divide by it.
  • Big amounts in crores.

Project 3.3 (Challenging): Customer feedback analysis

Find the main words and the mood in a pile of customer comments.

A restaurant group in Bengaluru collects short comments from customers after every meal. The manager reads a few, but there are far too many to read them all.

She wants a quick text summary: how many comments are positive, negative or neutral, which words customers use most (ignoring small words like "the" and "was"), and which positive and negative words came up. This is a small version of the "text analytics" that analysts run on reviews and survey answers.

What you will practise

  • Normalise text: lower case, split into words, strip punctuation
  • Count words with a dictionary and filter with sets
  • Score each comment and count the results
  • Rank words by count with an A to Z tie rule

You may use: everything from Parts 1 and 2, string methods, lists, tuples, sets and set operators, dictionaries, comprehensions, sorted() with key=.
Do not use yet: your own functions (def, lambda), try / except, import, files.

Your tasks

  1. The starter gives you three sets: STOP_WORDS, POSITIVE and NEGATIVE. Keep them as they are.
  2. In a loop, ask Comment (empty line to finish): and collect the comments. Stop at the first empty line (or a line with only spaces).
  3. If there were no comments, print No comments to analyse and stop.
  4. For each comment: make it lower case, split it into words, and strip . , ! ? from both ends of each word (word.strip(".,!?")). Drop words that become empty.
  5. Score each comment: +1 for every word in POSITIVE, −1 for every word in NEGATIVE. Above 0 is Positive, below 0 is Negative, 0 is Neutral.
  6. Count every word that is not in STOP_WORDS in a dictionary. Unique words is how many different words were counted.
  7. Top words: the 3 most common counted words (fewer if there are fewer), most common first. Equal counts go A to Z. Print them like food (3), service (2), clean (2).
  8. Print the positive and the negative words that were used, A to Z, joined with , (or none).

Lines your output must contain

Comments: <count>
Positive: <count>
Negative: <count>
Neutral: <count>
Unique words: <count>
Top words: <word> (<count>), <word> (<count>), <word> (<count>)
Positive words used: <word>, <word> or none
Negative words used: <word>, <word> or none
No comments to analyse

Counts are plain whole numbers. Words are always lower case. Lists of words are joined with a comma and one space. Print No comments to analyse instead of everything else when the first line is empty.

Edge cases to think about

  • The first line is empty: there is nothing to analyse.
  • Capitals and punctuation: Food!, food. and FOOD, are the same word.
  • Stop words never count, even if they are the most common.
  • Ties in the top words: A to Z.
  • Fewer than 3 different words: show only the ones there are.
  • No positive or negative words at all: print none.
