Part 3: Collections

Strings, lists, tuples, sets and dictionaries.

  1. 8. Strings in depth – Text is everywhere in data: names, cities, emails, product codes. Learn to pick out parts of a string, clean it up and check what it contains.
  2. 9. Lists – The list is Python's most used collection: an ordered, changeable row of items. Learn to add, remove, sort and copy, and avoid the copying trap that catches everyone.
  3. 10. Tuples – A tuple is a list that cannot be changed. Learn when that is exactly what you want, and master packing and unpacking.
  4. 11. Sets – A set is a bag of unique items with no order. It removes duplicates for free and compares groups in one line: who is in both lists, who is missing, who is new.
  5. 12. Dictionaries – A dictionary stores data as key: value pairs, so you find things by name instead of by position. It is the shape of most real data: a customer record, a product, a row from a database.
  6. 13. Comprehensions and choosing the right collection – Write loops that build lists in one clear line, then step back and compare strings, lists, tuples, sets and dictionaries so you always pick the right one.
  7. Part 3 overview – The five collections side by side: what each one is, when to use it, and the methods people confuse. Start with the big table, then work through the "don't mix these up" pairs.
  8. Part 3 projects – 3 real data-analyst problems with an automatic score out of 100.

Part 8: Pandas

Load, clean, select, group and join tables of data.

  1. 23. Pandas basics: Series, DataFrames and reading files – Meet the library every data analyst uses. Make Series and DataFrames, read CSV and Excel files, and give any new table a quick health check: its size, its types, its statistics, its gaps and its duplicates.
  2. 24. Cleaning data with Pandas – Turn a messy file into a clean table. Remove repeats, fill or drop gaps, drop what you do not need, fix types, tidy text with .str, translate values with a dictionary, and turn text into real dates.
  3. 25. Selecting, filtering and changing data – Ask a table questions. Pick rows and columns by label or position, filter with conditions, add new columns with maths and your own rules, sort, and find the top and bottom values.
  4. 26. Grouping, pivot tables and joining tables – Summarise and combine tables like an analyst. Group rows and summarise each group, add group results to every row, build pivot tables, put numbers into bands, join tables on a key and stack them, and measure how columns move together.
