Part 9 projects: charts with Matplotlib

Three reports a manager can see at a glance: a monthly sales chart, a two-chart sales overview, and a full 2 × 2 sales dashboard with a trend line. Pandas does the numbers, Matplotlib draws them, and the checker reads your charts: their type, counts, titles and labels.

In the interactive version you write each program in the browser, run it with your own numbers, and press Submit to get an automatic score out of 100 with feedback. A score of 70 or more means you are ready for the next project.

Project 9.1 (Beginner): Monthly sales chart

Total the orders by month, print the highlights and draw a clean line chart.

Rahul from Part 8 liked his monthly report, and now he wants a picture for the team meeting: one line chart of the sales in every month of the year, plus the best and worst month and the average. He will paste the saved picture into his slides.

What you will practise

  • Total sales per month with groupby and dt.to_period("M")
  • Draw a line chart with markers, a title and axis labels
  • Use short month names on the x-axis
  • Save the chart with savefig() before show()

You may use: everything from Parts 1-8, Matplotlib: plt.figure, plt.plot, plt.title, plt.xlabel, plt.ylabel, plt.grid, plt.savefig, plt.show.
Do not use yet: Seaborn (Part 10).

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv (Date, Category, Region, Sales, Profit) and turn Date into dates. If the file has no rows, print No orders in the file and stop (draw nothing).
  2. Total the Sales of each month: groupby(orders["Date"].dt.to_period("M")).
  3. Print Months: <n> (how many months have orders), Best month and Worst month with their totals (on a tie, the earlier month), and Average per month. Use the short month names: Jan, Feb ...
  4. Draw one line chart of the monthly totals with markers: the short month names along the x-axis, the title Monthly sales, the x label Month and the y label Sales (₹).
  5. Save it as monthly_sales.png, print Saved monthly_sales.png, then plt.show().

Lines your output must contain

Months: <n>
Best month: <Mon> (₹<amount>)
Worst month: <Mon> (₹<amount>)
Average per month: ₹<amount>
Saved monthly_sales.png
No orders in the file
Chart report (checked after your program): Figure 1: 1 chart
Chart 1.1: line, 1 line, title: Monthly sales, x: Month, y: Sales (₹)

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. The chart must be a single line (markers are fine; no extra lines or dots), with exactly the title and labels above.

Project 9.2 (Intermediate): Sales overview: two charts

Filter the orders by size, then draw sorted category bars and a region pie in one figure.

The sales head wants one picture for the monthly review: on the left, which categories bring in the most money; on the right, how the orders are shared between the regions. Sometimes she only wants the bigger orders, so the program first asks for the smallest order value to include.

What you will practise

  • Read and check a typed number, then filter rows with a mask
  • Group, sort and count with Pandas
  • Draw two charts in one figure with plt.subplots(1, 2)
  • Sort the bars, show percents on the pie, and give the figure one title

You may use: everything from Parts 1-8, Matplotlib: plt.subplots, ax.bar, ax.pie, ax.set_title, ax.set_ylabel, fig.suptitle, plt.tight_layout, plt.show.
Do not use yet: Seaborn (Part 10).

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv. Ask Smallest order (₹): . If it is not a whole number of 0 or more, print Please enter a whole number of rupees and stop.
  2. Keep the orders whose Sales is at least that value. If none are left, print No orders of ₹<value> or more (the value with commas) and Orders used: 0, and stop. Otherwise print Orders used: <n>.
  3. Print the Top category (biggest total sales; a tie: A to Z) with its total and its share of all the sales kept: Top category: Electronics (₹1,23,456.00 ... in the format of the lines below.
  4. Print the Busiest region (most orders; a tie: A to Z) with its number of orders and its share of the orders.
  5. Make one figure with 2 charts side by side (plt.subplots(1, 2)). Left: a bar chart of the total sales per category, largest first, with the title Sales by category and the y label Sales (₹).
  6. Right: a pie of the number of orders per region with the percent on each slice (autopct="%1.1f%%") and the title Orders by region.
  7. Give the figure the title Sales overview, call plt.tight_layout() and show it.

Lines your output must contain

Orders used: <n>
Top category: <Category> (₹<amount>, <x.x>% of sales)
Busiest region: <Region> (<n> orders, <x.x>%)
Please enter a whole number of rupees
No orders of ₹<value> or more
Chart report: Figure 1: 2 charts, title: Sales overview
Chart 1.1: bar, <n> bars, title: Sales by category, y: Sales (₹)
Chart 1.2: pie, <n> pie slices, title: Orders by region

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals; percents 1 decimal. The left chart has no x label; the pie has no axis labels. Only the two charts, no others.

Project 9.3 (Challenging): Sales dashboard

Four charts in one figure, with a trend line and the numbers a manager asks for.

Meena runs the analytics team. Every month she sends the directors one dashboard: monthly sales, profit by region, the spread of order values, and sales against profit with a trend line. Build it once, so next month she only changes the file.

What you will practise

  • Lay out a 2 × 2 grid with plt.subplots(2, 2) and axes[row, col]
  • Use four chart types: line, sorted horizontal bars, histogram and scatter
  • Fit a trend line with np.polyfit and report the slope and R²
  • Title every chart and the figure, tidy with tight_layout, and save the picture

You may use: everything from Parts 1-8 (NumPy included), all of Part 9: subplots, ax.plot, ax.barh, ax.hist, ax.scatter, ax.set_title, fig.suptitle, np.polyfit, np.corrcoef, savefig.
Do not use yet: Seaborn (Part 10).

Your tasks

  1. Read orders.csv and turn Date into dates. If it has fewer than 2 rows, print Need at least 2 orders and stop (no chart).
  2. Print Orders: <n>, the Best region for profit (highest total profit; a tie: A to Z) with its profit, and Loss-making orders: <n> (profit below 0).
  3. Fit a straight line of Profit against Sales with np.polyfit(x, y, 1). Print the slope as Profit per extra ₹1 of sales: ₹<x.xx> and R² (the squared correlation, np.corrcoef) as R²: <x.xx>.
  4. Make a 2 × 2 figure. Top left axes[0, 0]: monthly total sales as a line (title Monthly sales). Top right: total profit per region as horizontal bars sorted small to large (title Profit by region).
  5. Bottom left: a histogram of Sales with 10 bins (title Order values, x label Sales (₹)). Bottom right: a scatter of Sales (x) against Profit (y) with the trend line drawn on it as one line (title Sales vs profit).
  6. Give the figure the title Sales dashboard, call plt.tight_layout(), save it as dashboard.png, print Saved dashboard.png and show it.

Lines your output must contain

Orders: <n>
Best region for profit: <Region> (₹<amount>)
Loss-making orders: <n>
Profit per extra ₹1 of sales: ₹<x.xx>
R²: <x.xx>
Saved dashboard.png
Need at least 2 orders
Chart report: Figure 1: 4 charts, title: Sales dashboard
Chart 1.1: line, 1 line, title: Monthly sales

Chart 1.3: hist, 10 bars, title: Order values, x: Sales (₹)
Chart 1.4: line+scatter, 1 line, <n> points, title: Sales vs profit

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. The slope and R² have 2 decimals. Only the labels listed: no x or y labels on the other charts.
