Part 9 overview: Matplotlib

Both Matplotlib chapters on one page: which chart answers which question, the quick way and the object way side by side, the finishing touches, and the mix-ups that spoil a chart (bar vs hist, plot vs scatter, savefig after show).

The whole Part in 5 lines

  1. Every chart: plt.figure(figsize=...) (or fig, ax = plt.subplots()), draw, label (title, x, y, legend), tidy (grid, tight_layout), then show() or savefig().
  2. Pick the chart from the question: line for time, bar for categories, histogram or box plot for spread, scatter for two numbers, pie for a few shares, heatmap for many correlations.
  3. The object way names every chart: fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 2), axes[0, 1].bar(...), ax.set_title(...), and fig.suptitle(...) over them all.
  4. Clean charts get read: sorted bars, labels with units, a light grid, no top/right spines, bar_label for exact numbers and annotate for the one point that matters.
  5. Extras: twinx() for a second unit, np.polyfit(x, y, 1) for a trend line and R², inset_axes to zoom, rcParams for one look everywhere.

Key definitions

Matplotlib

Python's main charting library. Use its pyplot part, imported as plt.

Figure

The whole picture: the canvas that holds one or more charts. figsize is its size in inches.

Axes

One chart area inside a figure, with its own x-axis, y-axis and title.

Artist

Anything drawn on a figure: lines, bars, text, ticks.

Histogram

Counts how many values fall into each range (bin) of a number column. Its bars touch.

Bins

The ranges a histogram cuts the numbers into. bins=5 makes 5 equal ranges.

Scatter plot

One dot per row with one number on each axis: shows whether two numbers are related.

Colormap (cmap)

A colour scale that turns numbers into colours, like viridis or coolwarm.

Box plot

Median, quartiles (the box), whiskers and outlier dots of a column, often one per group.

IQR

Interquartile range: Q3 − Q1, the width of the box. Values beyond 1.5 × IQR from the box are outliers.

subplots

plt.subplots(rows, cols) gives a figure and an array of axes.

Spines

The four border lines of an axes; hide the top and right ones for a clean look.

twinx

A second axes sharing the x-axis, with its own y-axis on the right, for a second unit.

Trend line

The straight line that best fits a scatter, from np.polyfit(x, y, 1) (slope, intercept).

Don't mix these up

Don't mix up: plt.bar vs plt.hist

plt.bar(names, heights)plt.hist(values, bins)
You give itThe heights you worked outRaw numbers; it counts them
x-axisCategoriesA number line
BarsHave gapsTouch

Rule of thumb: Categories → bar. One number column → hist.

Don't mix up: plt.plot vs plt.scatter

plt.plot(x, y)plt.scatter(x, y)
DrawsA line joining the points in orderSeparate dots
Use it forChange over timeThe link between two numbers
Order of rowsMattersDoes not matter

Rule of thumb: Time on the x-axis? Line. Two measures of the same rows? Scatter.

Don't mix up: plt.title vs ax.set_title

plt.title("T")ax.set_title("T")
Works onThe current chartThe axes you name
In a grid of chartsEasy to label the wrong oneClear
Common errorax.title("T"): TypeError

Rule of thumb: In a grid, always use ax.set_title().

Don't mix up: plt.subplot vs plt.subplots

plt.subplot(1, 2, 1)fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2)
MakesOne chart at a timeAll the charts at once
Counts from10 (axes[0])
ReturnsOne axesThe figure and an array of axes

Rule of thumb: Use plt.subplots for dashboards: you get every axes by name.

Don't mix up: suptitle vs set_title

fig.suptitle()ax.set_title()
Belongs toThe whole figureOne chart
How manyOne per figureOne per axes

Rule of thumb: The figure has a supertitle; each chart has its own title.

Don't mix up: savefig before show, not after

savefig() then show()show() then savefig()
The fileThe chartAn empty picture
WhyThe chart still existsshow() finished the chart

Rule of thumb: Save first. Then show.

Try it yourself

Charts need Matplotlib and Pandas, which load the first time you press Run (about 10 to 20 seconds). The checker looks at your chart, not the pixels: the chart type, the counts, the titles and labels.

  1. From sales_data.csv, draw the average order value per category as sorted horizontal bars (biggest at the top), with a title and an x label.

    Hint

    groupby("Category")["Sales"].mean().sort_values(), then plt.barh(avg.index, avg.values).

    Solution

    Sorting small to large puts the biggest bar on top in a barh chart.

  2. Make a 1 × 2 figure: a histogram of Customer_Age with 12 bins (left) and a scatter of Sales against Profit (right), each with a title, and the figure title Customers and orders.

    Hint

    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 4)).

    Solution

    One figure, two questions: who buys, and do big orders pay.

  3. Draw the monthly total sales as a line, mark the best month with ax.annotate and an arrow, and hide the top and right spines.

    Hint

    Group by dt.to_period("M"), best = monthly.values.argmax().

    Solution

    A clean chart with one clear message: November.

Mixed quiz

  1. Which chart shows how monthly sales changed over the year?

    1. Pie
    2. Line
    3. Box plot
    4. Heatmap
    Answer

    Line. Change over time: a line.

  2. What does this print?

    1. (1, 3)
    2. (3,)
    3. 3
    4. (3, 1)
    Answer

    (3,). With one row, axes is a 1D array of 3 charts.

  3. Your x-axis has numbers on a continuous scale and the bars touch. Which chart is it?

    1. Bar
    2. Histogram
    3. Pie
    4. Scatter
    Answer

    Histogram. Histogram bars touch because the x-axis is a number line.

  4. Which line labels the y-axis of ax?

    1. ax.ylabel("Sales")
    2. ax.set_ylabel("Sales")
    3. plt.ax.ylabel()
    4. ax.label_y("Sales")
    Answer

    ax.set_ylabel("Sales"). Axes methods start with set_.

  5. What does this print?

    1. 2
    2. 3
    3. 1
    4. 5
    Answer

    2. One plt.bar call with two names draws two bars.

  6. What does a box plot's box show?

    1. The mean ± 1
    2. The middle half of the values: Q1 to Q3
    3. Every value
    4. Only the outliers
    Answer

    The middle half of the values: Q1 to Q3. The box is the IQR; the line inside is the median.

  7. How many slices are too many for a pie chart?

    1. More than 2
    2. More than about 5
    3. More than 20
    4. There is no limit
    Answer

    More than about 5. With many slices, a sorted bar chart is easier to read.

  8. Why use vmin=-1, vmax=1 in a correlation heatmap?

    1. To hide values
    2. So the colours always mean the same correlation, with 0 in the middle
    3. To round values
    4. It is required
    Answer

    So the colours always mean the same correlation, with 0 in the middle. Otherwise the scale stretches to this table's smallest and largest value.

  9. What does ax2 = ax1.twinx() give you?

    1. A copy of the chart
    2. A second y-axis on the right that shares the x-axis
    3. A second figure
    4. A legend
    Answer

    A second y-axis on the right that shares the x-axis. Use it for a second unit, like orders next to rupees.

  10. What does this print?

    1. [3. 2.]
    2. [2. 3.]
    3. [3. 1.]
    4. [1. 3.]
    Answer

    [3. 2.]. y = 3x + 2: slope 3, intercept 2.

  11. Which order saves a picture of the chart?

    1. plt.show() then plt.savefig()
    2. plt.savefig() then plt.show()
    3. Either
    4. plt.close() then plt.savefig()
    Answer

    plt.savefig() then plt.show(). show() finishes the chart; save first.

Flashcards

The 5-step recipe
figure → draw → label → tidy → show / savefig
Figure vs axes vs artist
Whole picture / one chart area / anything drawn
bar vs hist
bar: heights you give for categories. hist: counts it works out for a number column.
plot vs scatter
plot joins points in order (time). scatter draws separate dots (two numbers).
Grouped and stacked bars
Grouped: shift x by ± width/2. Stacked: bottom= the first series.
Reading a box plot
Box: Q1 to Q3. Line: median. Whiskers: 1.5 × IQR. Dots: outliers.
A grid of charts
fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 2); axes[row, col]; fig.suptitle(); plt.tight_layout()
Heatmap
imshow(corr, cmap="coolwarm", vmin=-1, vmax=1) + colorbar() + names on ticks + text per cell
Clean style
Hide top/right spines, light grid behind, sorted bars, labels with units.
Values and arrows
ax.bar_label(bars); ax.annotate("Peak", xy=..., xytext=..., arrowprops=...)
Second unit
ax2 = ax1.twinx(); merge legends with get_legend_handles_labels()
