Part 1 projects: Python basics

Three small programs that a data analyst could really be asked to write. They use only Part 1: print(), variables, input(), type conversion, f-strings and operators. No if, no loops and no lists yet.

In the interactive version you write each program in the browser, run it with your own numbers, and press Submit to get an automatic score out of 100 with feedback. A score of 70 or more means you are ready for the next project.

Project 1.1 (Beginner): Daily sales summary

Turn a day of juice sales into a clean ₹ report.

Meena runs a small juice shop in Guntur. She sells three drinks: Mango juice, Lemon soda and Sugarcane juice. Every evening she writes the units sold and the price of each drink in a notebook, then adds everything up by hand. It takes time, and sometimes the totals are wrong.

Write a program that asks for the numbers, does all the maths, and prints a clean daily sales report.

What you will practise

  • Read numbers typed by the user with input()
  • Convert text to int and float correctly
  • Do maths with *, + and /
  • Format money with an f-string: ₹1,305.00

You may use: print(), variables, input(), int() and float(), f-strings with :, and :,.2f, + - * /, * to repeat text.
Do not use yet: if / else, loops, lists, your own functions, import.

Your tasks

  1. Ask for 6 values with input(), in this order: Mango juice units, Mango juice price, Lemon soda units, Lemon soda price, Sugarcane juice units, Sugarcane juice price.
  2. Units are whole numbers, so convert them with int(). Prices can have paise (like 25.50), so convert them with float().
  3. Work out the revenue of each drink: units × price.
  4. Work out the total units sold and the total revenue.
  5. Work out the average revenue per product: the total revenue divided by 3 (there are always 3 drinks).
  6. Print a title line DAILY SALES REPORT, with a divider line above and below it made with * (for example "=" * 34).
  7. Print the report lines exactly as shown below. Money has a ₹, commas and 2 decimals (:,.2f). Total units has commas (:,).
  8. Add a few comments that explain your steps.

Lines your output must contain

DAILY SALES REPORT
Mango juice revenue: ₹<amount>
Lemon soda revenue: ₹<amount>
Sugarcane juice revenue: ₹<amount>
Total units sold: <units>
Total revenue: ₹<amount>
Average revenue per product: ₹<amount>

<amount> means money with commas and 2 decimals, like 2,400.00. <units> means a whole number with commas, like 1,250.

Example run

Typed values: 40, 60, 25, 30, 30, 25.5.

Mango juice units sold: 40
Mango juice price: 60
Lemon soda units sold: 25
Lemon soda price: 30
Sugarcane juice units sold: 30
Sugarcane juice price: 25.5
==================================
DAILY SALES REPORT
==================================
Mango juice revenue: ₹2,400.00
Lemon soda revenue: ₹750.00
Sugarcane juice revenue: ₹765.00
----------------------------------
Total units sold: 95
Total revenue: ₹3,915.00
Average revenue per product: ₹1,305.00

Edge cases to think about

  • A drink sold 0 units that day, or the shop was closed: the report still prints, with ₹0.00.
  • Prices with paise, like 25.50 or 19.99: use float(), because int("25.50") crashes.
  • A very busy day with big numbers, like 300,000 units: commas keep them readable.
  • Numbers typed with spaces around them, like 12 : int() and float() ignore those spaces.

Hint 1

Use one variable per value, with a clear name, for example mango_units = int(input("Mango juice units sold: ")). The text inside input() is just the question shown to the user.

Hint 2

Project 1.2 (Intermediate): Monthly KPI report

Turn four numbers into the KPIs a manager checks every month.

Ravi is a junior data analyst at a chain of electronics stores in Hyderabad. At the end of every month, his manager asks for the same KPIs (key performance indicators): profit, profit margin, growth compared with last month, and how close the store came to its target.

The team also gets a bonus when the store meets its target and keeps a profit margin of at least 15%.

Write a program that asks for four numbers and prints the whole KPI report, including True/False answers for the target and the bonus.

What you will practise

  • Turn a business question into formulas
  • Use brackets to control the order of operations
  • Format money and percentages with f-strings
  • Answer yes/no questions with comparison and logical operators

You may use: print(), variables, input(), float(), f-strings with :,.2f, :.2f and :.1%, + - * / and brackets, >=, >, and, or, not.
Do not use yet: if / else, loops, lists, your own functions, import.

Your tasks

  1. Ask for 4 values with input(), in this order: this month's revenue, this month's cost, last month's revenue, the monthly target. Convert each one with float().
  2. Profit = revenue − cost.
  3. Profit margin (%) = profit ÷ revenue × 100.
  4. Growth vs last month (%) = (revenue − last month's revenue) ÷ last month's revenue × 100. Use brackets.
  5. Target achieved = revenue ÷ target. Print it with the :.1% format, which multiplies by 100 for you.
  6. Target met = True when the revenue is at least the target (so exactly on target counts).
  7. Team bonus = True when the target is met and the profit margin is at least 15.
  8. Print a title line MONTHLY KPI REPORT and the report lines exactly as shown below. A loss prints as a minus amount, like Profit: ₹-60,000.00. That is fine: you cannot use if yet.

Lines your output must contain

MONTHLY KPI REPORT
Revenue: ₹<amount>
Cost: ₹<amount>
Profit: ₹<amount>
Profit margin: <number>%
Growth vs last month: <number>%
Target achieved: <percent>
Target met: <True or False>
Team bonus: <True or False>

<amount> means money with commas and 2 decimals (:,.2f), like 150,000.00. <number>% means 2 decimals then a % sign (:.2f), like 17.65%. <percent> uses the :.1% format, like 94.4%. <True or False> is the word True or False.

Example run

Typed values: 850000, 700000, 800000, 900000.

This month's revenue: 850000
This month's cost: 700000
Last month's revenue: 800000
Monthly target: 900000
================================
MONTHLY KPI REPORT
================================
Revenue: ₹850,000.00
Cost: ₹700,000.00
Profit: ₹150,000.00
Profit margin: 17.65%
Growth vs last month: 6.25%
Target achieved: 94.4%
Target met: False
Team bonus: False

Edge cases to think about

  • Allowed values: revenue, last month's revenue and the target are always more than 0 (the store was open). The cost can be 0 or more. So you never divide by zero.
  • A loss month (cost bigger than revenue): profit, margin and growth come out negative.
  • Revenue exactly equal to the target: the target is met. Use >=, not >.
  • A month with no cost: the margin is 100%.
  • Very big numbers (crores) and numbers typed with spaces.

Hints

Hint 1

Read all four numbers first: revenue = float(input("This month's revenue: ")), and the same for cost, last_revenue and target. Then work out one KPI per line, each in its own variable.

Hint 2

Brackets matter for growth: (revenue - last_revenue) / last_revenue * 100. Without them, Python divides first. For the yes/no answers, a comparison gives True or False that you can store: target_met = revenue >= target.

Hint 3

The bonus needs both conditions: team_bonus = target_met and margin >= 15. Print with f-strings: {profit:,.2f} for money, {margin:.2f}% for the margin, and {achieved:.1%} where achieved = revenue / target.

Project 1.3 (Challenging): Website session analytics

Turn raw website numbers into a summary the marketing team can read.

An online saree store in Chennai gets a raw export from its website tool every night: the total time all visitors spent on the site (in seconds), the number of sessions (visits), the data the pages used (in MB) and the number of orders.

Nobody can read "187530 seconds" at a glance. The marketing team wants a short daily summary with times in hours and minutes, data in GB, the conversion rate (the share of sessions that ended in an order), and a quick Healthy day flag: True when the conversion rate is at least 2% and an average session lasts at least 2 minutes.

What you will practise

  • Break seconds into hours, minutes and seconds with // and %
  • Convert units (MB to GB)
  • Work out rates and averages safely
  • Combine two conditions into one True/False flag

You may use: print(), variables, input(), int(), f-strings with :,, :.2f and :,.2f, + - * / // %, >=, and, or.
Do not use yet: if / else, loops, lists, your own functions, import.

Your tasks

  1. Ask for 4 whole numbers with input(), in this order: total time in seconds, number of sessions, data used in MB, number of orders. Convert each with int().
  2. Print Sessions and Orders with commas (:,).
  3. Total time: split the total seconds into hours, minutes and seconds with // and %, and print Total time: 52 h 5 m 30 s.
  4. Average session: total seconds // sessions (whole seconds, rounded down). Split it into minutes and seconds: Average session: 2 min 30 sec.
  5. Data used in GB = MB ÷ 1024, printed with :,.2f. Data per session = MB ÷ sessions, printed with :.2f.
  6. Conversion rate (%) = orders ÷ sessions × 100, printed with :.2f and a % sign.
  7. Healthy day = True when the conversion rate is at least 2 and the average session is at least 120 seconds.
  8. Print a title line WEBSITE DAILY SUMMARY and the report lines exactly as shown below.

Lines your output must contain

WEBSITE DAILY SUMMARY
Sessions: <count>
Orders: <count>
Total time: <h> h <m> m <s> s
Average session: <m> min <s> sec
Data used: <number> GB
Data per session: <number> MB
Conversion rate: <number>%
Healthy day: <True or False>

<count> uses commas (:,), like 1,250. In Data used, <number> has commas and 2 decimals (:,.2f). In the other lines, <number> has 2 decimals (:.2f). Hours, minutes and seconds are plain whole numbers. <True or False> is the word True or False.

Example run

Typed values: 187530, 1250, 3532, 45.

Total time on site (seconds): 187530
Number of sessions: 1250
Data used (MB): 3532
Number of orders: 45
==================================
WEBSITE DAILY SUMMARY
==================================
Sessions: 1,250
Orders: 45
Total time: 52 h 5 m 30 s
Average session: 2 min 30 sec
Data used: 3.45 GB
Data per session: 2.83 MB
Conversion rate: 3.60%
Healthy day: True

Edge cases to think about

  • Allowed values: sessions is always at least 1, so dividing by it is safe. The other values can be 0.
  • Only 1 session: the average session is the whole time.
  • A day with 0 orders: the conversion rate is 0.00%.
  • Exactly on the limits (2% and 120 seconds): that counts as healthy, so use >=.
  • A huge day: thousands of hours and millions of MB, with commas where the format says so.
  • Numbers typed with spaces around them.

Hints

Hint 1

For the total time: hours = total_seconds // 3600. The seconds left after taking out whole hours are total_seconds % 3600, and // 60 on that gives the minutes. total_seconds % 60 gives the seconds.

Hint 2

The average session uses floor division so it stays whole: average_seconds = total_seconds // sessions. Then split it the same way: average_seconds // 60 minutes and average_seconds % 60 seconds.

Hint 3

Store the flag in a variable: healthy_day = conversion_rate >= 2 and average_seconds >= 120. Then print it with an f-string, like print(f"Healthy day: {healthy_day}").
