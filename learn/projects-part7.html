Part 7 projects: NumPy for analysis

Three analysis jobs where NumPy does the heavy lifting: a weekly sales summary, a store-by-month sales matrix, and a discount what-if table built with broadcasting. Use arrays and vectorised maths instead of loops wherever you can.

Project 7.1 (Beginner): Weekly sales with NumPy

Summarise a week of sales with arrays, argmax and a boolean mask.

Sunita manages a bakery in Kochi. Every Sunday night she types the 7 daily sales totals of the week into her laptop and wants a quick summary: the total, the average, the best and worst day, which days beat the average, and how much of the week's money came in at the weekend.

Write it with NumPy: put the 7 numbers in an array and let NumPy do the maths, with no loops for the calculations.

What you will practise

  • Turn typed text into a NumPy array
  • Use sum(), mean(), argmax() and argmin()
  • Filter with a boolean mask
  • Check the input and handle bad values

You may use: everything from Parts 1-6, numpy arrays and their methods, boolean masks, try / except.
Do not use yet: pandas (not in this course yet).

Your tasks

  1. The starter imports NumPy and gives you DAYS, the 7 day names from Monday to Sunday.
  2. Ask Sales for Mon to Sun (7 numbers): and split the line at the spaces. If there are not exactly 7 parts, print Please enter exactly 7 numbers and stop.
  3. Convert the parts with float() inside try. If one is not a number, print Please enter numbers only and stop.
  4. Make a NumPy array of the 7 values. Print Total and Average.
  5. Print the best day and the worst day with their sales, using argmax() and argmin() (on a tie, the earlier day).
  6. Print the days whose sales are above the average, using a boolean mask on an array of the day names (or none).
  7. Print the weekend share: Saturday + Sunday as a percent of the total, with 1 decimal. If the total is 0, the share is 0.0%.

Lines your output must contain

Total: ₹<amount>
Average: ₹<amount>
Best day: <Day> (₹<amount>)
Worst day: <Day> (₹<amount>)
Days above average: <Day>, <Day> or none
Weekend share: <percent>%
Please enter exactly 7 numbers
Please enter numbers only

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. The weekend share has 1 decimal and a % sign. Day names are the 3-letter names from DAYS.

Example run

Typed values: 1200 1350 980 1500 1720 2400 2100.

Sales for Mon to Sun (7 numbers): 1200 1350 980 1500 1720 2400 2100
Total: ₹11,250.00
Average: ₹1,607.14
Best day: Sat (₹2,400.00)
Worst day: Wed (₹980.00)
Days above average: Fri, Sat, Sun
Weekend share: 40.0%

Edge cases to think about

  • Not 7 numbers, or a word in the list.
  • Two days with the same best or worst sales: the earlier day.
  • Every day the same: no day is above the average.
  • A week with no sales: the total is 0, so the share must not divide by it.

Project 7.2 (Intermediate): Store sales matrix

Load a stores-by-months table into a 2D array and analyse it with axis.

Vikram is the regional analyst for a chain of mobile shops in Hyderabad. Each quarter he gets the sales of every store for January, February and March, and his manager always asks the same questions: what was the total, which month was best, how do the stores rank and what share does each bring, and which stores grew every single month?

Build the answers from one 2D NumPy array: one row per store, one column per month. Most answers are one line with axis=.

What you will practise

  • Build a 2D array from typed rows
  • Use sum(axis=0) and sum(axis=1) correctly
  • Rank rows by their totals
  • Test a condition on every month with np.diff and np.all

You may use: everything from Parts 1-6, NumPy arrays, axis, masks, np.diff, np.all, argmax, sorted() with key=.
Do not use yet: pandas (not in this course yet).

Your tasks

  1. The starter imports NumPy and gives you MONTHS = ["Jan", "Feb", "Mar"].
  2. Ask How many stores? . It must be a whole number from 1 to 10; otherwise print Number of stores must be 1 to 10 and stop.
  3. Then ask for each store's row: name, jan, feb, mar. A row is invalid if it does not have 4 parts, the name is empty, or a sales value is not a number: print Invalid row, try again and ask for that store again.
  4. Put the sales in a 2D array (stores × 3 months) and keep the names in a list. Print Total sales.
  5. Print the monthly totals (axis=0) like Monthly totals: Jan ₹X, Feb ₹X, Mar ₹X, and the best month (the first one on a tie).
  6. Rank the stores by their total (axis=1), highest first; equal totals keep the order they were typed. Print 1. Ameerpet: ₹X (34.5%) with each store's share of the total sales (0.0% if the total is 0).
  7. Print the stores that grew every month (Jan < Feb < Mar), using np.diff(..., axis=1) and np.all(..., axis=1), or none.
  8. Print the average sales per store per month (the mean of the whole array).

Lines your output must contain

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. Shares have 1 decimal and a % sign. Store names are printed without the spaces around them.

Project 7.3 (Challenging): Discount what-if planner

Use broadcasting to test every discount on every product in one go.

Before Diwali, a kirana and gifts store in Vijayawada wants to choose a discount. A bigger discount sells more units but earns less on each one. The owner wants a what-if table: for each discount level, the total revenue and profit, which discount gives the most revenue, which gives the most profit, and which products stay profitable at every discount.

Assume each 1% discount sells 1.5% more units (ELASTICITY), and each unit costs 60% of its normal price (COST_SHARE). With NumPy broadcasting, a column of discounts and a row of products become a full table without a single loop over the numbers.

What you will practise

  • Parse structured input and validate every part
  • Turn a 1D array into a column with [:, None] for broadcasting
  • Build scenario × product tables with vectorised maths
  • Summarise along an axis and pick the best scenario with argmax

You may use: everything from Parts 1-6, NumPy arrays and broadcasting, axis, argmax, np.all, functions and try / except.
Do not use yet: pandas (not in this course yet).

Your tasks

  1. The starter gives ELASTICITY = 1.5 and COST_SHARE = 0.6. Keep them.
  2. Ask Products (name:price:units, ...): , split at commas, then each product at : (strip every part). Each needs a name, a price above 0 and units of 0 or more; otherwise print Invalid product: <the product text> and stop.
  3. Ask Discounts in % (for example 0 5 10): and split at spaces. Each must be a whole number from 0 to 90; otherwise print Invalid discount: <text> and stop. If there are none, print Please enter at least one discount.
  4. Make arrays: prices and units (one value per product) and discounts (one per scenario). Make d = discounts[:, None] / 100, a column.
  5. With broadcasting: new price = prices × (1 − d); new units = units × (1 + ELASTICITY × d); revenue = new price × new units; profit = (new price − prices × COST_SHARE) × new units. Each is a table: one row per discount, one column per product.
  6. Print one line per discount with the totals of its row (sum(axis=1)): Discount 10%: revenue ₹X, profit ₹Y.
  7. Print the best discount for revenue and for profit (argmax; the first one on a tie).
  8. Print the products whose profit is above 0 at every discount (np.all(profit > 0, axis=0)), or none.

Lines your output must contain

Discount <d>%: revenue ₹<amount>, profit ₹<amount>
Best discount for revenue: <d>%
Best discount for profit: <d>%
Profitable at every discount: <names> or none
Invalid product: <text>
Invalid discount: <text>
Please enter at least one discount

Money has ₹, commas and 2 decimals. Discounts are whole numbers printed with a % sign. Product names are printed without the spaces around them.

Example run

Edge cases to think about

  • A product with a missing part (Tea:20), a word as the price, or a price of 0.
  • A discount that is a word, a decimal, above 90 or negative.
  • A big discount where the new price falls below the cost: the profit turns negative.
  • Only one discount: it is the best for both.
  • Spaces everywhere in the input.
